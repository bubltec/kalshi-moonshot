Confidence score study: does a curve-feature model know something the market price does not? Script: scripts/confidence_study.py (pure Python logistic, ridge 1.0, run on the host). Crypto 15-minute windows (the windows table has strikes for those only), marks at 300, 180 and 90 s before the close, 11,288 rows from the last 9 days; walk-forward over the last three days (train on the days before each test day).
Models: M0 market mid (logistic on logit(mid)); M1 digital price from the spot's own variance; M2 logit(mid) + curve features (distance to strike, 60 s drift, bounce off 5-minute extremes, path efficiency, 15-minute range and position, the hour's move, where the strike and spot sit in the hour's range, time left).

Out-of-sample log loss (lower is better): market 0.3027, digital 0.3128, model 0.3016 (pooled, 2,874 rows). By fold: day 1 market 0.3013 / model 0.3039; day 2 0.3008 / 0.2949; day 3 (120 rows) 0.3409 / 0.3558. The model beats the market in 1 of 3 folds.
Buying the side the model likes when it beats that side's ask by at least EDGE, after the fee, held to settlement, EV per contract: EDGE 0.03: 533 buys +0.0385 (halves +0.0157 / +0.0615); EDGE 0.06: 201 buys +0.0525 (+0.0573 / +0.0477); EDGE 0.10: 48 buys -0.0016 (+0.0193 / -0.0242).
Coefficients (last fit, standardised): logit_mid 1.05, z_tm -0.45, pos15 +0.28, h_pos +0.17, up5 +0.16, dn5 +0.14, h_move -0.14, left -0.13, amp15 -0.12, h_strike -0.10.
Read: the model adds almost nothing on average (log loss 0.3016 vs 0.3027) but its disagreements of 3-6 cents with the price were positive in both halves of the test. Three test days, three overlapping marks per window, features chosen by hand: not established. Existing bot score for comparison (1,957 settled live bets, Brier): the ask beats model_p on nearly every lane (crypto_favorite 0.145 vs 0.158; commodity_energy 0.100 vs 0.127); cal_p is about equal to the ask.
