# kalshi-moonshot: rules for studies, rules and changes

## How we study and decide (applies everywhere in this repo)

1. **All buying and selling logic is based on curve variance.** Every entry, exit, stake and gate reads how the market's own curve is behaving, in units of its own variance (typical minutes: the root-mean-square one-second move times sqrt(60)), not in absolute cents, basis points or dollars. **Classify by curve, never by asset.** No rule, gate, split or study may be keyed on a coin, commodity or market name. A market is described by what its curve is doing: the measures in `src/kalshi_moonshot/marketprofile.py` (typical-minute size, crossings, range, late flips, path efficiency) and, per bet, the spot's path efficiency, bounce and drift over the minutes before the decision. Asset names may label a log line or a report, nothing more. When a result looks like "it works on crypto", the cause is a curve shape; find the shape.
2. **Thresholds come from the market's own recent history.** A cutoff is a quantile of that market's last hours of readings, recomputed on a schedule (hourly for ticks in memory, daily for the tape). A fixed number is allowed only for something structural (the fee, the close time, a settlement window).
3. **Anomalies are their own state.** A reading outside its market's recent range (beyond its 1st/99th percentile, or volatility over three times its median) is logged as `anomaly`, is never counted as choppy or straight, and no rule is assumed to hold there. The rule sits out and the reading is recorded so the state can be studied.
4. **A study asks one question on the same bets.** Compare against what actually happened on those bets (held to settlement, or the lane's own exit). Include the fee on every leg. Report n, the net change in dollars, how many bets the rule beat and lost to what happened, and the result by curve shape, where the shapes are the features the curve model itself identified (its level, slope and curvature readings at each window; see `curvemodel.py`), not a split by time or by asset. A rule needs a positive result in each curve shape it claims before it goes to shadow. (User, 2026-11-12: "the halves strategy is not a good one ... it should be by curve shape, what features it identified".) Settings chosen after seeing the results are said to be so.
5. **Shadow first, then live.** A new rule logs "would do X" with its curve reading, is scored against the bets that followed, and runs for at least a week of live bets before anyone turns it on. The live switch is the user's. Paper before real money.
   **Breaking fixes skip the week** (user, 2026-11-08). When a live bet shows the book is not working as it should (a missing exit, a rule that does not apply to a lane it should, a sale or buy that did not happen), the fix is made live at once, with the evidence on the bet that broke and the nearest study in the commit message and the lane comment. It is a fix, not a new rule: the week of shadow and the both-halves bar are for new ideas. A fix is still logged with its curve reading, scored against the bets that follow, and retired under rule 6 if its score turns negative. Whether a change is a fix is the user's call.
6. **Rules retire.** Every shadow rule keeps a rolling score against what happened. A rule whose score turns negative over its window is flagged in the log and stops being proposed.
7. **Keep the evidence.** Scripts in `scripts/`, results in `studies/results/` (dated), the question and the answer in the commit message. A claim made in chat is backed by a file here or it is marked as unverified.
8. **The main strategy is the state record.** A lane buys a curve state because that state's own recorded result paid (the shrunk net per dollar staked, after the fee, over enough earlier markets at the buy's price band), not because a person picked it. States are curve readings (varieties at several scales), never a coin, a commodity or a market. The record is rebuilt from the tape on a schedule (`src/kalshi_moonshot/staterecord.py`, buy step `state_record`). A lane that runs against this (a hand-picked variety or price rule, a long-shot scalp with no record behind it) is retired or changed unless it was positive over the last 36 hours. Code that serves only a retired rule is removed, not left dormant.

## Architecture rules (decided by the user; a change or cleanup keeps them)

- **Entry and exit run on the websocket, not on timers** (user, 2026-11-14: "we need to get rid of these timers"; 2026-11-18: "the whole app should be running on the socket"; 2026-11-19: "nothing should be running off a timer unless its a background job for database calculations"). The design the user laid out on 2026-11-19:
  - The websocket subscribes to everything: markets, market changes (lifecycle), tickers, trades, the order book, the account's fills and the settlement indexes.
  - **Ticks trigger entry logic for all watched lanes** (`entrywatch.py`): the yes/no evaluation, then the buy module's steps in order. The thread keeps no rule; the lane's filter and the steps decide.
  - **Ticks trigger sell logic for every lane with an active buy or buy order** (`watcher.py`): the exit module's steps decide. A change in price re-evaluates a resting buy order and may update it.
  - **The perp websocket runs for value comparisons.** It hints at direction and gives every market a cushion in real numbers (the index against the target, in the index's own typical moves) to compare with, in place of Kalshi's percent cushion, which does not make sense on every market.
  - The only timers are background jobs that build or refresh data (history, resolve, account sync, the state record, templates). A lane the stream drives has no review or scope timer; the timers come back only while the feed is down or the entry thread is dead (`Worker._driven`, `EntryWatcher.alive`). Deleting or bypassing the entry thread is a change to this rule and needs the user's word.
- **Work is branched into queues and threads that talk through shared memory** (user, 2026-11-19: "everything should be branched into queues and threads that communicate with each other on shared memory"). The socket reader only stores the tick and wakes the threads that care: the entry thread (ticks to buy decisions), the watcher thread (ticks to sell decisions), a buy-order thread (placing, re-quoting and cancelling resting buys as the price moves), the in-memory store of quotes, books, bets and watchlists they share, and background jobs that build templates into that memory. A queue between threads is in memory; the database is the persisted copy that is written behind them, not the channel they use. (Built 2026-11-19: each (lane, event) has its own thread and queue in `entrywatch.py`; the evaluation runs on it with no job row (`Worker.evaluate_shot`; the order id comes from `orderkeys.py`); a resting buy waits on the socket's fills and quotes (`restbuys.py`). The database job queue remains only for lanes on timers while the feed is down. Still inline: the bet's own decision row, its order row and the bet book rebuild are written right after the order, and a lane's evaluations take `lane_lock` through a resting wait.)
- **Latency and isolation** (user, 2026-11-19: "maintain a low latency for logic in both buying and selling (under 60ms to evaluate per tick -- each market should have its own thread so nothing should be blocked)"). One tick's buy decision and one tick's sell decision each take under 60 ms, measured (`steptime`), and each market runs on its own thread (or a worker bound to that market), so a slow market, a slow order or a slow lane never delays another. A step that cannot meet 60 ms reads a precomputed template instead of computing on the tick. (Measured and held 2026-11-19: `steptime.tick` times the sell tick, the entry review and the evaluation and logs `tick latency` against the 60 ms budget; `memguard.py` logs, and in tests raises on, any SELECT or Kalshi GET inside a buy or sell decision; `tests/test_architecture_rules.py` replays ticks against both. The first host numbers after this change are the proof; the baseline before it was a median of 1006 ms per evaluation.)
- **No database call anywhere in buy or sell logic** ("db calls should not exist on the buy or sell logic, those should only occur in background jobs", 2026-11-19; earlier, user, 2026-11-16: "Nothing in per tick computations should do a db read. Ever. We can do structured background jobs to build a template for a market but never per tick"; 2026-11-19: "no db calls should happen and all calculations should come from in-memory store"). A tick, a review, a buy step and a sell step read the in-memory tape, the in-memory bet book and the in-memory watchlist (`Store.watch`, `store.watched_in_memory`). A reading that needs stored history is built by a background job into a per-market template and read from memory. Writes (logging a decision, an order) are queued and never waited on. `tests/test_architecture_rules.py` fails the build on a timer or a table read in these paths.

## Working rules

- **Buying and selling happen only in modules** (user, 2026-11-19: "we don't use anything outside modules for buying and selling"). A lane buys through its `buy_module` (steps in `buychain.py`) and sells through its `exit_module` (steps in `sellchain.py`). The lane says what it looks at, how big a bet is and how the order is placed. A new rule is a step, never a lane setting that acts on its own.

- Query the Docker Postgres, not `data/moonshot.db` (stale). Live runs in the cloud (us-east-2 host through SSM); the Mac runs config and analysis.
- `data/strategies.toml` is untracked and shipped by `scripts/cloud_push.sh`. A config that names a new lane field needs the new image: use `--build`. An old image refuses an unknown field.
- A restart (`--build`) lands just after a quarter-hour close: start the push at about xx:44:40, xx:14:40 and so on, after a warm build.
- Do not add attribution lines to commit messages or pull request descriptions.
